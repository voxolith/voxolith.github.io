The renderer holds no environment state of its own. Every frame, `render(params)` takes the camera
and every lighting field in [`FrameParams`](/docs/renderer/api/renderer/interfaces/FrameParams/),
so a static scene passes constants and a day/night cycle passes whatever its clock says. Point
lights and materials are uploaded separately, because they change rarely.

## The key light and the sky

One directional **key light** lights the scene:

| field | meaning |
|---|---|
| `lightDir` | direction towards the key light (unit vector) |
| `lightColor` | its colour and strength, linear RGB |
| `ambientSky`, `ambientGround` | hemispheric ambient: sky above, ground bounce below, blended by the normal |

A hit is shaded with the hemispheric ambient, the key light's diffuse term (with a soft fill from
the camera side, so the shadowed half does not go muddy), face ambient occlusion and the point
lights. The key light casts one shadow ray per pixel from just outside the hit face, capped by the
quality preset's `shadowSteps`; `low` turns it off. **Ambient occlusion** is classic voxel face AO:
the empty cell in front of the hit face is darkened by its solid neighbours along the two tangent
axes and the corners, 8 lookups per hit (`ao` in the quality preset).

The **sky** is a vertical gradient (`skyTop`, `skyHorizon`) with blocky stars at night
(`nightFactor`) and world-positioned sun and moon discs (`sunDir`, `moonDir`, `sunColor`,
`moonColor`, `sunIntensity`, `moonIntensity`). The discs only paint the sky: they light nothing.
The key light, its colour and the ambient do all the lighting, so a moonlit night is a dim blue
`lightColor` along `moonDir`, not the moon disc.

Turning a time of day into these values is not the renderer's job.
[`timeOfDay(phase)`](/docs/engine/api/atmosphere/functions/timeOfDay/) in
[`@voxolith/engine/atmosphere`](/docs/engine/atmosphere/) returns every lighting field from one
number (0 midnight, 0.5 noon), so animating a transition is animating `phase`.

```ts
import { timeOfDay } from "@voxolith/engine/atmosphere";

renderer.render({ ...camera(orbit.yaw(), orbit.distance(), orbit.target(), orbit.pitch()), ...timeOfDay(0.42) });
```

## Point lights

On top of the key light the scene has up to `MAX_LIGHTS` (32) point lights: a lamp, a lantern, a lit
window that lights what is around it. The list is the app's; upload it with `setLights`.

```ts
renderer.setLights([
  { position: [120, 30, 80], color: [1, 0.7, 0.4], intensity: 2.5, range: 90, glow: 3 },
  { position: [60, 20, 40], color: [0.4, 0.6, 1], range: 40, shadows: false },
]);
loop.invalidate();
```

<TypeTable
  type={{
    position: { description: 'Grid-space position, in voxels.', type: '[number, number, number]', required: true },
    color: { description: 'Linear RGB.', type: '[number, number, number]', required: true },
    intensity: { description: 'Multiplies color.', type: 'number', default: '1' },
    range: { description: 'Voxels. The light contributes exactly nothing beyond this.', type: 'number', required: true },
    shadows: { description: 'Trace a shadow ray towards the light.', type: 'boolean', default: 'true' },
    glow: { description: 'Radius of the visible halo, in voxels. 0 draws none.', type: 'number', default: '0' },
  }}
/>

- **`range` is hard.** Past it a light contributes exactly nothing, and the range test comes first,
  so a pixel no light reaches costs a loop over the list and nothing else.
- **Falloff.** Inside the range the light fades smoothly to zero at the edge (a window times a soft
  inverse square), so it ends without a ring.
- **Shadows.** `shadows` (on by default) traces one shadow ray per pixel towards the light. The ray
  stops at the light, so a lamp above the ground is not shadowed by ground far behind it, and it
  follows the quality preset's `shadowSteps`: `low` keeps the lights and drops their shadows. The
  cost is one short ray per shadowed light in range, per pixel. An occluded light still contributes
  a little, so a stair-stepped voxel edge in its shadow does not read as a black hole.
- **Glow.** `glow` draws a visible halo along the view ray, so the lamp itself shows, including
  against the sky. A light behind the surface the ray hit shows nothing.
- **Reflections.** Metal and glass reflections pick up the lights, unshadowed.
- **Cost of updating.** `setLights` rewrites a 1.5 KB buffer, so moving a lamp every frame is cheap.
  Invalidate the frame loop afterwards.

<Callout title="Emissive voxels do not light their surroundings">
An emissive material only lights its own voxel. To make a lamp light the room, put a point light at
it as well.
</Callout>

## Materials

Materials are per palette slot. Without them the renderer shades a flat palette, and a scene that
never uploads materials renders byte-for-byte as it did before they existed. Pass them as
`RenderScene.materials`, or later with `updateMaterials(materials)`: 256 slots of 8 floats,
`[kind, rough, metal, emit]` then `[ior, alpha, att, spec]`.

| kind | value | shading |
|---|---|---|
| diffuse | 0 | the default |
| metal | 1 | a single reflection bounce, optionally roughened, tinted by the voxel's colour |
| glass | 2 | transmitted and refracted past the voxel, tinted, with a Fresnel reflection; `alpha` 0 is clear |
| emit | 3 | adds `colour × emit` of self-glow |
| water | 4 | an animated, transparent surface; see below |

Reflections and refraction are one bounce: the pass traces a lit secondary ray and does not recurse.

You rarely pack this buffer by hand. Entities declare a
[`MaterialHint`](/docs/engine/api/engine/interfaces/MaterialHint/) per colour role, and the
engine's `PaletteAllocator.buildMaterials()` (world slots) or `instancePalette` (instance palettes)
packs them. [`packMaterials(scene)`](/docs/renderer/api/renderer/functions/packMaterials/) packs a
MagicaVoxel scene's `MATL` chunks.

### Water

Water is a material kind, not a separate system. Voxels whose material is `water` stay voxels, so
water streams, clips and edits like everything else; only its shading differs:

- Top faces get a rippled normal from a few travelling waves, animated by `FrameParams.time`
  (seconds). Nothing is re-uploaded to make it move; a constant `time` freezes it.
- From that normal the surface reflects the scene or the sky, refracts down to the bed (found by a
  second ray walk that treats water as empty), and blends the two by Fresnel.
- The bed fades into the water's own colour with depth (`att`, per voxel) and catches moving
  caustics; the sun and point lights leave glints on the ripples.
- `waterWind` in the [atmosphere](/docs/renderer/atmosphere/) drifts and raises the ripples.

An app with water on screen must render continuously for it to move; see
[Quality and performance](/docs/renderer/quality-and-performance/#render-on-demand).

## Floor and highlight

Two optional extras are drawn on ray miss and at shading time:

- `setFloor({ enabled, y, colorA, colorB })` draws an infinite blocky ground plane for rays that miss
  the grid going down, so a model floats over a floor that extends past its box. Games whose ground
  is real voxels leave it off.
- `setHighlight({ mode, slotMin, slotMax })` spotlights a selection: with `mode` 1, voxels whose
  palette slot is in the range keep their colour and get a bright rim on their silhouette, and the
  rest greys out.
