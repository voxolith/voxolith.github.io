The renderer draws raw atmospheric effects and knows nothing about weather. Fog, a cloud layer,
falling particles, wet or covered ground and wind on the water are independent effects, each off
until its amount is above zero, so a scene that sets none of them renders exactly as before.

What the effects *mean* (rain, a blizzard, a foggy morning) is decided above the renderer:

| layer | owns |
|---|---|
| renderer | the effects: optional fields of `FrameParams`, all off at zero |
| [`@voxolith/engine/atmosphere`](/docs/engine/atmosphere/) | the vocabulary and translator: `timeOfDay`, `Atmosphere` presets, blending and transitions, `approach`, `atmosphereFrame` |
| the game | whether there is weather at all, when it changes, how wetness and snow cover build up |

Keep policy out of the engine and meaning out of the renderer.

## The fields

The effects are the optional half of
[`FrameParams`](/docs/renderer/api/renderer/interfaces/FrameParams/), typed as
[`AtmosphereParams`](/docs/renderer/api/renderer/interfaces/AtmosphereParams/). They are sent every
frame with the camera and lighting; nothing is uploaded.

### `fog`

Exponential extinction towards a colour, optionally thinning with height. It is applied to hits by
their distance and to the sky at a fixed far distance, so the horizon dissolves into it. Lamp glows
are seen through the fog between the eye and each lamp.

`distance` turns fog into a view distance ("fog of war"): primary rays stop there, and the fog
closes to total over its last quarter, so the cut never shows and nothing past it is traced. The
sky is behind the wall too. It is the way to bound a close-up scene at a fine scale, like the
[nightwood](/docs/examples/nightwood/) example.

### `clouds`

A drifting noise layer over the sky gradient. `cover` 1 is overcast and hides the sun, moon and
stars.

<TypeTable
  type={{

    color: { description: 'Cloud colour.', type: '[number, number, number]', required: true },
    drift: { description: 'Drift of the layer, sky units per second on x and z.', type: '[number, number]', required: true },
  }}
/>

### `precipitation`

Rain streaks or snowflakes as analytic particles: nothing is stored or uploaded, and the field
covers a world of any size. The primary ray samples a handful of depth layers, near to far,
stopping at whatever it hit, so roofs and trees occlude the particles for free. At each sample,
space is sheared along the fall direction (so streaks lean with the wind), stretched for rain,
scrolled by fall × time and cut into cells, and a hash puts at most one particle in each cell.
World positions stay put as the camera moves, and particles are kept at least a pixel wide at
distance.

<TypeTable
  type={{
    kind: { description: 'Streaks or flakes.', type: '"rain" | "snow"', required: true },
    density: { description: '0 to 1: how many particles.', type: 'number', required: true },
    fall: { description: 'Fall velocity in voxels per second; wind tilts it.', type: '[number, number, number]', required: true },
    color: { description: 'Particle colour, already lit for the scene.', type: '[number, number, number]' },
  }}
/>

The particles move with `FrameParams.time`, so precipitation needs a continuously rendering loop.

### `surface`

The ground's state rather than the sky's. `wet` darkens the ground and adds a sheen to upward
faces; `cover` blends upward faces towards `coverColor` (settled snow). On medium and high quality a
short upward ray keeps the cover off ground under canopies and roofs; `low` skips that ray.

<TypeTable
  type={{

    cover: { description: '0 to 1: upward faces blend to coverColor.', type: 'number', required: true },

  }}
/>

### `waterWind`

`[x, z]`: drifts the ripples on [water](/docs/renderer/lighting/#water); its length also raises
them.

### `effectScale`

How many voxels the effects' own sizes are measured in (default 1): water wavelengths and bed fade,
rain and snow particle size and layering, the sky's fog distance and the snow-shelter test. A world
at k times the resolution passes k so waves, caustics, drops and snowflakes keep their size in it.
Fog density and fall speeds are already per voxel and are scaled by whoever sets them.
`atmosphereFrame(..., { voxelsPerMetre })` sets all of this for you.

## Cost

| effect | cost per pixel |
|---|---|
| fog, clouds | a few arithmetic operations |
| precipitation | 8 hash samples along the primary ray |
| surface cover | one short upward ray per upward-facing pixel, while `cover > 0` (medium and high only) |
| wet, waterWind | shading terms only |

## From the engine

A game that wants weather keeps an `Atmosphere` (a preset, a script, a season), changes it by its
own rules and hands it to `atmosphereFrame` with the time-of-day lighting each frame. The result is
spread straight into `render`:

```ts
import { atmosphereFrame, ATMOSPHERES, timeOfDay } from "@voxolith/engine/atmosphere";

const weather = { ...ATMOSPHERES.rain, wetness: 0.6 }; // the game owns wetness and cover
renderer.render({
  ...camera(orbit.yaw(), orbit.distance(), orbit.target(), orbit.pitch()),
  ...atmosphereFrame(timeOfDay(0.4), weather, { voxelsPerMetre: 10 }),
  time: now / 1000,
});
```

See [Atmosphere](/docs/engine/atmosphere/) in the engine section for the presets, blending and
transitions.
