The renderer draws raw atmospheric effects and knows nothing about weather. Fog,
falling particles, wet or covered ground and wind on the water are independent effects, each off
until its amount is above zero, so a scene that sets none of them renders exactly as before.

What the effects *mean* (rain, a blizzard, a foggy morning) is decided above the renderer:

| layer | owns |
|---|---|
| renderer | the effects: optional fields of `FrameParams`, all off at zero; a slot for a background module |
| [`@voxolith/engine/atmosphere`](/docs/engine/atmosphere/) | the vocabulary and translator: `timeOfDay`, `Atmosphere` presets, blending and transitions, `approach`, `atmosphereFrame`; the skies (`classicSky`, `makePhysicalSky`) |
| the game | whether there is weather at all, when it changes, how wetness and snow cover build up |

Keep policy out of the engine and meaning out of the renderer.

## The fields

The effects are the optional half of
[`FrameParams`](/docs/renderer/api/renderer/interfaces/FrameParams/), typed as
[`AtmosphereParams`](/docs/renderer/api/renderer/interfaces/AtmosphereParams/). They are sent every
frame with the camera and lighting; nothing is uploaded.

### `fog`

Exponential extinction towards a colour, optionally thinning with height. It is applied to hits by
their distance and to the sky at a fixed far distance, so the horizon dissolves into it. Lamp glows
are seen through the fog between the eye and each lamp.

`distance` turns fog into a view distance ("fog of war"): primary rays stop there, and the fog
closes to total over its last quarter, so the cut never shows and nothing past it is traced. The
sky is behind the wall too. It is the way to bound a close-up scene at a fine scale, like the
[nightwood](/docs/examples/nightwood/) example.

### `precipitation`

Rain streaks or snowflakes as analytic particles: nothing is stored or uploaded, and the field
covers a world of any size. The primary ray samples a handful of depth layers, near to far,
stopping at whatever it hit, so roofs and trees occlude the particles for free. At each sample,
space is sheared along the fall direction (so streaks lean with the wind), stretched for rain,
scrolled by fall × time and cut into cells, and a hash puts at most one particle in each cell.
World positions stay put as the camera moves, and particles are kept at least a pixel wide at
distance.

The particles move with `FrameParams.time`, so precipitation needs a continuously rendering loop.

### `surface`

The ground's state rather than the sky's. `wet` darkens the ground and adds a sheen to upward
faces; `cover` blends upward faces towards `coverColor` (settled snow). On medium and high quality a
short upward ray keeps the cover off ground under canopies and roofs; `low` skips that ray.

### `waterWind`

`[x, z]`: drifts the ripples on [water](/docs/renderer/lighting/#water); its length also raises
them.

### `effectScale`

How many voxels the effects' own sizes are measured in (default 1): water wavelengths and bed fade,
rain and snow particle size and layering, the sky's fog distance and the snow-shelter test. A world
at k times the resolution passes k so waves, caustics, drops and snowflakes keep their size in it.
Fog density and fall speeds are already per voxel and are scaled by whoever sets them.
`atmosphereFrame(..., { voxelsPerMetre })` sets all of this for you.

## Backgrounds

The renderer draws no sky of its own. What a ray shows when it hits nothing is a **background**: a
WESL module handed to `createRenderer` and linked into the shader, so it is chosen at creation and
cannot be swapped later. Its parameters change per frame.

```ts
import { classicSky, classicSkyParams, timeOfDay } from "@voxolith/engine/atmosphere";

const renderer = await createRenderer(gpu, scene, { background: classicSky });
const light = timeOfDay(0.42);
renderer.render({ ...camera(35), ...light, background: classicSkyParams(light) });
```

- [`RendererOptions.background`](/docs/renderer/api/renderer/interfaces/RendererOptions/) takes a
  [`BackgroundOptions`](/docs/renderer/api/renderer/interfaces/BackgroundOptions/): `source`, the
  module, and `textures`, up to two filterable 2D float texture views.
- [`FrameParams.background`](/docs/renderer/api/renderer/interfaces/FrameParams/) is the module's
  parameters for the frame: up to 64 floats (`BACKGROUND_FLOATS`), zero past what is given.
- **The default** is `GRADIENT_BACKGROUND_WESL`, a plain two-colour sky: one colour at and below
  the horizon, blending to another towards the zenith. No sun, stars or clouds.
  `gradientBackgroundParams(horizon, zenith, out?)` packs its colours; without
  `FrameParams.background` it draws `GRADIENT_BACKGROUND_DEFAULTS`, a hazy daylight blue.
- **Skies** with a sun, moon, stars and clouds are the engine's:
  [`classicSky` and `makePhysicalSky`](/docs/engine/atmosphere/#skies).

### Writing one

A background module exports one function and may import the renderer's `bg` module:

```wgsl
import package::bg::{BG, bgTex0, bgTex1, bgSampler};

fn background(rd : vec3<f32>, footprint : f32) -> vec3<f32> {
  return mix(BG.params[0].xyz, BG.params[1].xyz, smoothstep(0.0, 0.6, max(rd.y, 0.0)));
}
```

- `rd` is the unit ray direction in grid space, y up; the result is linear RGB.
- `footprint` is the angular size of what the ray stands for, in radians: one pixel for primary
  rays, twice that for reflections. Use it to anti-alias small things such as stars and discs.
- `BG.params` is `array<vec4<f32>, 16>`, the frame's `background` floats; `BG.time` is
  `FrameParams.time`.
- `bgTex0` and `bgTex1` are the two textures (1 × 1 black when not given), and `bgSampler` filters
  linearly, repeating in u and clamped in v, for longitude-latitude maps. Sample with
  `textureSampleLevel`: the background runs in compute kernels and inside non-uniform control
  flow, where `textureSample` is not allowed.
- Keep it small. It is called from several places in the shader (primary misses, reflections, the
  floor's fade), and a software adapter's compile time grows with every inlined copy.

Renderers with the same background source share one linked shader.

<Callout title="Changed: the sky left FrameParams">
  `FrameParams` no longer has `sunDir`, `moonDir`, `sunColor`, `moonColor`, `skyTop`,
  `skyHorizon`, `nightFactor`, `sunIntensity`, `moonIntensity` or `clouds`. A page that passed them
  gets the default gradient until it creates its renderer with `background: classicSky` and passes
  the packed parameters, which `atmosphereFrame` already returns as `background`.
</Callout>

## Cost

| effect | cost per pixel |
|---|---|
| fog | a few arithmetic operations |
| background | whatever its module does, per ray that leaves the world; the default gradient is one `mix` |
| precipitation | 8 hash samples along the primary ray |
| surface cover | one short upward ray per upward-facing pixel, while `cover > 0` (medium and high only) |
| wet, waterWind | shading terms only |

## From the engine

A game that wants weather keeps an `Atmosphere` (a preset, a script, a season), changes it by its
own rules and hands it to `atmosphereFrame` with the time-of-day lighting each frame. The result is
spread straight into `render`:

```ts
import { atmosphereFrame, ATMOSPHERES, classicSky, timeOfDay } from "@voxolith/engine/atmosphere";

const renderer = await createRenderer(gpu, scene, { background: classicSky }); // the sky atmosphereFrame describes
const weather = { ...ATMOSPHERES.rain, wetness: 0.6 }; // the game owns wetness and cover
renderer.render({
  ...camera(orbit.yaw(), orbit.distance(), orbit.target(), orbit.pitch()),
  ...atmosphereFrame(timeOfDay(0.4), weather, { voxelsPerMetre: 10 }),
  time: now / 1000,
});
```

See [Atmosphere](/docs/engine/atmosphere/) in the engine section for the presets, blending and
transitions.
