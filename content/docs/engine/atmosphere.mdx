Time of day and weather are layered in three:

- the **renderer** draws raw atmospheric effects (`fog`, `clouds`, `precipitation`, `surface`,
  `waterWind` in [`FrameParams`](/docs/renderer/api/renderer/interfaces/FrameParams/), all off at
  zero) and knows nothing about weather ([Atmosphere](/docs/renderer/atmosphere/) under Renderer);
- the **engine**, `@voxolith/engine/atmosphere`, is the vocabulary and the translator: a way to
  describe the sky, presets, blending and transitions, and one function that turns time of day
  plus an atmosphere into the renderer's settings;
- the **game** decides whether there is weather, when it changes, and how wetness and snow cover
  build up.

Keep policy out of the engine and meaning out of the renderer. The module has no simulation and no
clock of its own, and it is optional: a game without weather never imports it.

```ts
import { atmosphereFrame, ATMOSPHERES, timeOfDay } from "@voxolith/engine/atmosphere";

renderer.render({ ...camera, ...atmosphereFrame(timeOfDay(0.5), { ...ATMOSPHERES.rain, wetness: 0.7 }), time });
```

## Time of day

[`timeOfDay(phase, options?)`](/docs/engine/api/atmosphere/functions/timeOfDay/) returns the
lighting half of `FrameParams` (everything but the camera) as a
[`Lighting`](/docs/engine/api/atmosphere/interfaces/Lighting/): key light direction and colour, sky
and ground ambient, sun and moon directions, colours and intensities, sky top and horizon, and the
night factor.

- `phase` runs 0 to 1 over a day: 0 midnight, 0.25 sunrise, 0.5 noon, 0.75 sunset.
- The sun rises in +X and sets in -X on a tilted arc towards +Z, as at mid latitudes: the noon sun
  stands about 55° up and off to one side, so walls are lit rather than only roofs. The moon is
  opposite.
- By day the key light follows the sun (never lower than a flattering elevation). At night it
  becomes a dim blue moonlight, and the ambient drops low enough for point lights to carry the
  scene. Around dusk both keys fade out together, so the hand-over is invisible.
- [`TimeOfDayOptions`](/docs/engine/api/atmosphere/interfaces/TimeOfDayOptions/): `nightAmbient`
  (0.12; 0 pitch black, 1 as bright as day) and `moonlight` (0.35, relative to sunlight).

Animate a transition by animating `phase`; the sky passes through sunset on the way. The moon disc
lights nothing itself: `lightDir`, `lightColor` and the ambients do.

## Describing weather

An [`Atmosphere`](/docs/engine/api/atmosphere/interfaces/Atmosphere/):

| field | range | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `cloud` | 0 to 1 | clear to overcast |
| `precipitation` | `{ kind, intensity }` | `kind` is `"none"`, `"rain"` or `"snow"`; intensity 0 to 1 |
| `wind` | `{ direction, strength }` | degrees the wind blows towards (0 = +Z, 90 = +X), strength 0 to 1 |
| `fog` | 0 to 1 | mist on top of whatever the precipitation brings |
| `wetness` | 0 to 1 | how wet the ground is |
| `cover` | 0 to 1 | how much snow has settled |

`wetness` and `cover` are the ground's state rather than the sky's. They build up and fade over
time by the game's rules; presets leave them at 0.

[`ATMOSPHERES`](/docs/engine/api/atmosphere/variables/ATMOSPHERES/) has presets: `clear`, `cloudy`,
`overcast`, `rain`, `storm`, `snow`, `blizzard`, `fog`.

## Changing it

- [`blendAtmosphere(a, b, t)`](/docs/engine/api/atmosphere/functions/blendAtmosphere/) mixes two.
  A change of precipitation kind fades one out before the other fades in (no sleet halfway between
  rain and snow), and the wind turns the short way round.
- [`makeAtmosphereTransition(start?)`](/docs/engine/api/atmosphere/functions/makeAtmosphereTransition/)
  returns an [`AtmosphereTransition`](/docs/engine/api/atmosphere/interfaces/AtmosphereTransition/):
  `set(target, seconds = 4)` eases towards a new atmosphere from wherever it is now (so a change of
  mind mid-transition does not jump), `update(dt)` returns the current one, and `changing()` says
  whether to keep rendering.
- [`approach(value, target, rate, dt)`](/docs/engine/api/atmosphere/functions/approach/) moves a
  value towards a target by at most `rate * dt`, never past it. It is for a game's own
  accumulators: rain soaking the ground, snow settling, things drying.

```ts
const weather = makeAtmosphereTransition(ATMOSPHERES.clear);
let wetness = 0;

function frame(dt: number, phase: number) {
  if (forecastSaysRain()) weather.set(ATMOSPHERES.rain, 8);
  const atm = weather.update(dt);
  const raining = atm.precipitation.kind === "rain" ? atm.precipitation.intensity : 0;
  wetness = approach(wetness, raining > 0 ? 1 : 0, raining > 0 ? 0.05 : 0.01, dt);
  renderer.render({ ...camera, ...atmosphereFrame(timeOfDay(phase), { ...atm, wetness }), time });
}
```

## Into the renderer

[`atmosphereFrame(lighting, atmosphere, options?)`](/docs/engine/api/atmosphere/functions/atmosphereFrame/)
returns the lighting plus the renderer's [`AtmosphereParams`](/docs/renderer/api/renderer/interfaces/AtmosphereParams/)
for one frame:

- Cloud blocks the direct key light and turns it into sky light: overcast dims and flattens the
  key light and greys the sky, keeping the night dark. Rain clouds are darker than white ones.
- Fog takes the horizon's colour; precipitation adds some fog of its own (snow more than rain).
- Wind tilts the rain and snow, drifts the clouds and drives the water (`waterWind`).
- Precipitation particles are lit like the scene, mostly ambient with some key light.
- `wetness` and `cover` become the renderer's `surface` settings.
- With a clear atmosphere the lighting passes through unchanged.

[`AtmosphereFrameOptions`](/docs/engine/api/atmosphere/interfaces/AtmosphereFrameOptions/):
`coverColor` for settled snow, `rainSpeed` (70 voxels per second) and `snowSpeed` (8), and
`voxelsPerMetre`.

### Scale

Everything is tuned for 10 voxels per metre. `atmosphereFrame(lighting, atmosphere, { voxelsPerMetre: 100 })`
gives the same weather in a finer world, in metres: fog per voxel thins, rain and snow fall more
voxels per second, and the renderer's `effectScale` keeps waves and drops their size.

<Callout title="Continuous rendering">
  Rain, snow and water animate from `FrameParams.time`, so an app with weather or water on screen
  renders continuously. With a still camera under vsync, pass `retryAfterMs: Infinity` to
  `makePerf` and call `perf.reprobe()` when the view moves; see
  [Quality and performance](/docs/renderer/quality-and-performance/).
</Callout>
