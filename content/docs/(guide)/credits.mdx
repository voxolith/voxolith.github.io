Voxolith stands on published graphics research. This page credits the work its techniques draw
on, says what each paper contributed and what Voxolith does differently, and lists further reading
that shapes where the renderer goes next. When a new technique lands, its papers are added here,
on the pages that describe it, in the package README, in the code and in the commit that brings it.

## Posing animated voxels on the GPU

Rigged instances ([engine: stamping and crowds](/docs/engine/stamping-and-crowds/#posed-on-the-gpu),
[renderer: instances](/docs/renderer/instances/#posed-on-the-gpu)) draw a creature from one rest
model shared by every copy: each frame only its bone matrices change, and the renderer takes each
world cell back through every bone's inverse into the rest model.

<Cards>
  <Card
    title="Ray Tracing Massive Amounts of Animated Geometry"
    href="https://doi.org/10.1145/3820014"
    description="Holger Gruen, Carsten Benthin, Michael Kern, David McAllister (AMD). Proc. ACM Comput. Graph. Interact. Tech. 9(4), Article 49 (HPG 2026). Third place, Wolfgang Straßer Award."
  />
  <Card
    title="Voxel Deformation-Aware Neural Intersection Function"
    href="https://doi.org/10.2312/egs.20261026"
    description="Chih-Chen Kao, Grzegorz Makowski, Shin Fujieda, Takahiro Harada (AMD). Eurographics 2026 Short Papers. arXiv:2604.24666."
  />
  <Card
    title="Real-time Rendering of Animated Meshless Representation"
    href="https://doi.org/10.2312/hpg.20251172"
    description="Pacôme Luton, Thibault Tricard. High-Performance Graphics 2025."
  />
</Cards>

- **Gruen et al.** animate a coarse tetrahedral cage instead of the geometry inside it, and transform
  each ray entering a deformed tetrahedron back into the static rest pose, so the dense geometry and
  its acceleration structure never change and are shared by every animated copy. Voxolith takes
  that principle: move the samples, not the geometry, and share one rest representation between all
  instances. ([author's version](https://gpuopen.com/download/TetrahedralMeshes_AuthorsVersion.pdf))
- **Kao et al.** map rays in deformed space back to a voxelised rest space voxel by voxel, with the
  deformation approximated as locally linear inside each voxel, to train a neural intersection
  function that works across poses. Voxolith uses the non-neural half of that idea: sampling the
  posed shape through a per-part inverse into the rest voxels.
  ([paper](https://gpuopen.com/download/egs20261026.pdf))
- **Luton and Tricard** encase meshless representations, including 3D textures (voxel grids), in a
  coarse rigged tetrahedral mesh to animate them; Gruen et al. build on it. It is the origin of the
  cage idea for voxels, and the reference for smooth (non-rigid) deformation if Voxolith adds it.

**What is Voxolith's own:** the deformation is rigid per bone, because a generated creature binds
each voxel to exactly one bone, so no cage is needed; sampling runs over the renderer's brick index
at world voxel centres, exactly reproducing what baking a pose on the CPU writes (checked cell for
cell in `engine/tools/verify-animation.ts`); each creature is one entry in the per-cell instance
lists, with a small grid of 32-bit part masks per 8³ brick so a sample tests one or two bones and a
ray skips the empty bricks of the creature's box; joints are welded as baking does; and the rig's
cover (fur over flesh a pose uncovers) is precomputed into the rest model.

## Tight occupancy for instance cells

The [sub-cell tables](/docs/renderer/instances/#crowded-cells) of static instance cells list, per
16³ sub-cell, only the instances that can draw a voxel there, so a ray through a dense wood tests
two or three instances a step and skips the bricks none of them reaches.

<Cards>
  <Card
    title="Subspace Culling for Ray–Box Intersection"
    href="https://doi.org/10.1145/3585503"
    description="Atsushi Yoshimura, Takahiro Harada (AMD). Proc. ACM Comput. Graph. Interact. Tech. 6(1) (I3D 2023)."
  />
</Cards>

- **Yoshimura and Harada** observe that a bounding box is a loose fit for what it holds, so many
  rays that hit the box miss everything inside, and cull those rays against a coarse occupancy of
  sub-volumes within each box. Voxolith took the idea and the analysis: measure how much of the
  traversal is spent in space that only a loose box claims, and mark tight occupancy instead. On
  nightwood, marked from turned model brick boxes, 58% of the fine steps were in such space.
- **Their technique itself was tried and not kept.** A sub-cell mask per box with a ray-mask table
  was implemented and measured on nightwood: over the tables below it saved another 2.5 to 8
  points at 20, 50 and 100 voxels per metre, for 4, 44 and 245 MiB of masks, and masks on world
  bricks made the world page 4 to 12% slower.

**What is Voxolith's own:** the tables are marked from each model's occupied 2³ sub-cells, turned
into world space, so a brick is marked and an instance listed only where a world voxel centre can
sample one of them; and every listed static cell gets a table, not only crowded ones. With the
shader unchanged, that took nightwood's frame down by 29 to 47% depending on the scale.

## Temporal accumulation of shadows

[Temporal accumulation](/docs/renderer/quality-and-performance/#temporal-accumulation)
(`RenderQuality.temporal`) keeps each pixel's shadows and AO across frames, so a still view stops
tracing them and the key light's shadows turn soft.

<Cards>
  <Card
    title="Combining Analytic Direct Illumination and Stochastic Shadows"
    href="https://doi.org/10.1145/3190834.3190852"
    description="Eric Heitz, Stephen Hill, Morgan McGuire. Proceedings of the ACM SIGGRAPH Symposium on Interactive 3D Graphics and Games (I3D 2018)."
  />
</Cards>

- **Heitz et al.** evaluate direct light analytically without shadows, estimate the shadowed share
  stochastically, and apply the ratio of the two, so the noisy estimate only carries visibility.
  Voxolith takes two things from it: accumulating a stochastic, jittered shadow visibility over
  frames into soft shadows, and, for point lights, keeping the ratio of shadowed to unshadowed light
  and applying it to the unshadowed light evaluated every frame, so a light that changes colour or
  brightness keeps its history.

**What is Voxolith's own:** the estimate is accumulated per pixel across frames rather than
denoised within one; history is rejected without motion vectors, by a hash of the voxel face each
pixel hit and its distance, reprojected through the previous camera; and what changes the shading
of a face a pixel still sees (edits, moving instances, their shadows) is reported as change stamps
on 8-voxel columns, swept along each shadowing light, which make those pixels trace afresh. When
most of the world changes in one frame, that frame renders without accumulation.

The key light's jitter follows the R2 low-discrepancy sequence from Martin Roberts,
[*The Unreasonable Effectiveness of Quasirandom Sequences*](https://extremelearning.com.au/unreasonable-effectiveness-of-quasirandom-sequences/)
(2018, blog post).

Papers from [AMD's publications](https://gpuopen.com/learn/publications/) that fit the renderer's

  Real-time Global Illumination.* 2022.
  [PDF](https://gpuopen.com/download/publications/GPUOpen2022_GI1_0.pdf),
  [arXiv:2310.19855](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.19855)

  Illumination.* SIGGRAPH Asia 2021 Technical Communications.

  Reflections Using Ray Tracing and Two-Level Radiance Caching.* SIGGRAPH Asia 2023.
  [PDF](https://gpuopen.com/download/publications/SA2023_RealTimeReflection.pdf)
