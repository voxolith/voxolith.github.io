title: Atmosphere and weather
description: Time of day, fog, clouds, rain, snow and wet or snowy ground - what the renderer draws, what the engine translates, and what your game decides.
---

Weather in Voxolith is split across three layers, and keeping them apart is what makes it easy to
change:

- The **renderer** draws raw atmospheric effects: `fog`, `clouds`, `precipitation`, `surface`
  (wet and snow-covered ground) and `waterWind`, all optional fields of `FrameParams` and all off at
  zero. It knows nothing about weather.
- The **engine** (`@voxolith/engine/atmosphere`, optional) is the vocabulary and the translator:
  `timeOfDay(phase)`, `Atmosphere` presets, blending and transitions, `approach`, and
  `atmosphereFrame(lighting, atmosphere)`, which turns them into the renderer's fields. It has no
  simulation and no clock of its own.
- The **game** decides whether there is weather at all, when it changes, and how wetness and snow
  cover build up and fade.

## One frame

```ts
import { atmosphereFrame, ATMOSPHERES, timeOfDay } from "@voxolith/engine/atmosphere";

renderer.render({
  ...frame(),
  ...atmosphereFrame(timeOfDay(0.5), { ...ATMOSPHERES.rain, wetness: 0.7 }),
  time: now / 1000,
});
```

`timeOfDay(phase)` runs 0 to 1 over a day: 0 midnight, 0.25 sunrise, 0.5 noon, 0.75 sunset.
Animate `phase` for a day passing; the sky goes through sunset on the way. Options: `nightAmbient`
(how dark the night is, default 0.12) and `moonlight` (default 0.35 of sunlight).

An `Atmosphere` is `cloud` (0 clear to 1 overcast), `precipitation` (`none`, `rain` or `snow`, with
an intensity), `wind` (direction in degrees, 0 towards +Z, and strength), `fog`, and the ground's
`wetness` and snow `cover`. `ATMOSPHERES` has presets: `clear`, `cloudy`, `overcast`, `rain`,
`storm`, `snow`, `blizzard`, `fog`. `atmosphereFrame` dims and flattens the key light under
overcast, greys the sky while keeping the night dark, gives fog the horizon's colour, and lets the
wind tilt the rain and drive the clouds and the water. Clear weather passes the lighting through
unchanged.

## Changing weather over time

Wetness and cover are the ground's state, not the sky's, so presets leave them at 0 and your game
keeps its own accumulators. The world example's rules, written with `approach`:

```ts
import { approach, atmosphereFrame, ATMOSPHERES, makeAtmosphereTransition, timeOfDay, type AtmosphereName } from "@voxolith/engine/atmosphere";

const sky = makeAtmosphereTransition(ATMOSPHERES.clear);
let wetness = 0, cover = 0, phase = 0.35;

function setWeather(name: AtmosphereName) {
  sky.set(ATMOSPHERES[name], 4); // eased over 4 s, from wherever it is now
  loop.setContinuous(true);
}

function render(now: number, dt: number) {
  phase = (phase + dt / 600) % 1; // a ten-minute day
  const atm = sky.update(dt);
  const p = atm.precipitation;
  const rain = p.kind === "rain" ? p.intensity : 0;
  const snow = p.kind === "snow" ? p.intensity : 0;
  // Rain soaks the ground and it dries slowly; snow settles and melts in rain or sun.
  wetness = rain > 0 ? approach(wetness, 1, 0.12 * rain, dt) : approach(wetness, 0, 0.02, dt);
  cover = snow > 0 ? approach(cover, 0.9, 0.05 * snow, dt) : approach(cover, 0, rain > 0 ? 0.05 : 0.012, dt);
  renderer.render({ ...frame(), ...atmosphereFrame(timeOfDay(phase), { ...atm, wetness, cover }), time: now / 1000 });
}
```

Those rates are the example's choices, not the engine's. A game with seasons, a forecast or a
scripted storm replaces them; `blendAtmosphere(a, b, t)` mixes two atmospheres directly when you
drive the blend yourself.

## Finer worlds

The presets are tuned for 10 voxels per metre. In a finer world pass the scale, and the same weather
comes out in metres: fog per voxel thins, rain and snow fall more voxels per second, and the
renderer's `effectScale` keeps waves, drops and snowflakes their size.

```ts
const atm = atmosphereFrame(timeOfDay(0.5), ATMOSPHERES.rain, { voxelsPerMetre: 100 });
```

## What the renderer draws, and its cost

- `fog`: exponential extinction towards a colour, optionally thinning with height; applied by
  distance to hits and to the sky, so the horizon dissolves. A few operations per pixel.
- `clouds`: a drifting noise layer over the sky; `cover` 1 is overcast and hides the sun, moon and
  stars. A few operations per pixel.
- `precipitation`: rain streaks or snowflakes as analytic particles along each primary ray, stopped
  by whatever the ray hit, so roofs and trees keep the rain off. Never uploaded and unlimited in
  extent; 8 hash samples per pixel.
- `surface`: `wet` darkens the ground and adds a sheen to upward faces; `cover` blends upward faces
  towards a snow colour. On medium and high quality one short upward ray per upward pixel keeps
  snow off ground under canopies and roofs; `low` skips it.
- `waterWind`: drifts and raises the ripples on [water](/docs/manual/water/).

You can set these fields directly, without the engine's module, for a fixed look: a foggy dungeon
needs only `fog`.

## Pitfalls

- Weather animates. While it is changing or falling, render continuously
  (`loop.setContinuous(true)`), and go back to on-demand once `sky.changing()` is false and nothing
  falls.
- A still camera in a continuously rendered scene can make an adaptive render scale hunt; see
  `retryAfterMs` in [Performance](/docs/manual/performance/).
- The moon disc lights nothing. Night lighting is the dim key light and ambient from `timeOfDay`,
  plus your point lights.
