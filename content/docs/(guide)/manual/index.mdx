The manual is organised by task. Each page answers one question you will have while making a
game: how do I do this, which parts of Voxolith are involved, what does it cost, and what goes
wrong. Pages are short and link out to the [API reference](/docs/renderer/api/) and to the
concept pages of each package when you need the full picture.

If you have not drawn anything yet, start with [Getting started](/docs/getting-started/) and the
[tutorial](/docs/tutorial/). The manual assumes you have a canvas, a `GpuContext` from `initGpu`
and a `Renderer` from `createRenderer`.

## How the pieces fit

Voxolith is three layers, and the manual follows their split:

- **`@voxolith/renderer`** draws. It holds the world as bricks, draws instances, lights and
  atmospheric effects, and knows nothing about entities, weather or gameplay.
- **`@voxolith/engine`** is the vocabulary between them: entities and colour roles, palettes,
  placement, streaming, instances, input, animation and atmosphere presets.
- **Generators** (`@voxolith/gen-*`) bake models: trees, rocks, buildings, creatures, terrain.

Your game decides what happens and when. Where the engine offers a helper for a policy (a phone
profile, a weather rule, a quality setting), it is a suggestion the game is free to replace.
