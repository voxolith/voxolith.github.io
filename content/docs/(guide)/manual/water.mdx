---
title: Water
description: Make water with the water material - ripples, Fresnel reflection, refraction to the bed, depth fade, caustics and wind - and keep it animating.
---

Water in Voxolith is a material, not a separate system. Voxels whose slot has the `water` material
ripple over time, reflect the scene by Fresnel, refract down to the bed (found by a second walk
that treats water as empty), fade into the water's colour with depth and catch moving caustics.
Because it stays voxels, water streams, clips, edits and carves like everything else.

## Making water

Give a role the `water` material hint and allocate it like any other role:

```ts
import { PaletteAllocator } from "@voxolith/engine";

const palette = new PaletteAllocator(1);
const { base } = palette.allocate([
  { id: "sand", name: "Sand", color: [0.76, 0.7, 0.5] },
  { id: "water", name: "Water", color: [0.12, 0.3, 0.38], material: { kind: "water", alpha: 0.35, att: 0.08 } },
], "pond");

const renderer = await createRenderer(gpu, { size, palette: palette.buildPalette(), materials: palette.buildMaterials() });
```

- `color` is the colour the water fades into with depth.
- `alpha` is its opacity, 0 clear to 1.
- `att` is how fast the bed fades with depth, per voxel.
- `rough` and `spec` shape the reflection.

Then fill the flooded cells with `base + 1` (the water role) up to the water level. Generated
terrain does this for you: `@voxolith/gen-terrain` fills every flooded column with its `WATER` role,
which already carries `{ kind: "water", alpha: 0.35, att: 0.18, rough: 0.2, spec: 0.8 }`.

## Keeping it moving

Water animates from `FrameParams.time`, in seconds. Pass the clock every frame:

```ts
renderer.render({ ...frame(), ...lighting, time: now / 1000 });
```

A constant `time` freezes the ripples. That has a consequence for render-on-demand apps: with water
on screen the picture changes every frame, so the app has to render continuously. The world
example does, and its `?still` option passes `time: 0` and renders on demand instead, which is also
what you want for screenshots and tests.

`waterWind` in `FrameParams` drifts the ripples and raises them with its length.
`atmosphereFrame` sets it from the atmosphere's wind, so a storm roughens the river on its own.

## Costs

A water pixel does more work than a plain one: a reflection, a second walk down to the bed and the
caustics. A lake filling the screen therefore costs more than the same area of grass. The quality
presets and the adaptive render scale apply to it like everything else.

In a world at more voxels per metre, pass `effectScale` (or `atmosphereFrame(..., { voxelsPerMetre })`)
so wavelengths and the bed fade keep their size in metres.

## Pitfalls

- Pass `materials` (from `buildMaterials()`, or `updateMaterials` later). Without a material block
  the renderer uses its flat palette path and water is an opaque colour.
- Water under instances works (an instanced tree reflects and shades as any voxel does), but
  instances only fill cells the world leaves empty, so a model standing in a pond needs the water
  cells under it cleared.
- A continuously rendered still camera can make `makePerf` resample the image as it probes;
  pass `retryAfterMs: Infinity` and call `perf.reprobe()` when the view moves.
