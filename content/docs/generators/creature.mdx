`@voxolith/gen-creature` makes rigged, animated creatures: a rat first, with other small quadrupeds
to follow from the same shape and different parameters. The entity is a rest-pose model whose voxels
each know their bone, a rig, and a set of generated clips. Play, blend and bake them with
[`@voxolith/engine/animation`](/docs/engine/animation/).

```ts
import { seededRandom } from "@voxolith/renderer/core";
import { generateCreature, PRESETS } from "@voxolith/gen-creature";
import { bakePose, makeAnimator, poseMatrices } from "@voxolith/engine/animation";

const { entity } = generateCreature(PRESETS.rat, seededRandom(1));
const anim = makeAnimator(entity, "walk");

// every frame:
anim.update(dt);
const posed = bakePose(entity.model, entity.rig!, poseMatrices(entity.rig!, anim.pose()), { yaw });
```

For many animated creatures, bake through the engine's pose cache and crowd rather than one
`bakePose` per frame each; see [stamping and crowds](/docs/engine/stamping-and-crowds/).

## Rig and clips

The rig has 26 bones: pelvis, spine, chest, neck, head, two ears, a seven-bone tail, and upper,
lower and foot for each of the four legs (`FL`, `FR`, `HL`, `HR`, exported as `LEGS`). Each bone
rotates about its head; the model binds every voxel to one bone through `model.bones`.

<ImageZoom src="/generators/creature-clips.webp" alt="Frames of the walk, turn-left, turn-right, run, idle, sniff and death clips" width={1600} height={1125} />

The clips are generated from the `gait` parameters (`stride`, `pace`, `bounce`, `tailSway`):

| clip | loop | notes |
|---|---|---|
| `walk` | yes | legs in diagonal pairs, a small bob, a `foot.<leg>` event at each footfall |
| `turn-left`, `turn-right` | yes | the walk with the spine, neck and head yawed into the turn and the tail swung out of it |
| `run` | yes | a bounding gallop with the spine flexing; `foot.front` and `foot.hind` events |
| `idle` | yes | 3 s of breathing, bursts of sniffing and an ear twitch |
| `sniff` | yes | 2 s rearing up on the hind legs and looking around |
| `death` | no | a roll onto the side, going limp |

Clips are keyed at 12 frames per second, like sprite animation: it suits voxels, where a limb only
moves a whole voxel or so between keys anyway, and lets a host cache baked poses per frame. Loops
repeat their first key at the end, so they close exactly. Rotations are quaternions relative to rest,
about each bone's head, in its parent's frame; clips can also carry a root offset (a bob, a crouch)
and named events.

## Inside

<ImageZoom src="/generators/creature-cut.webp" alt="A rat cut open along its length, showing fur, fat, flesh, bone and organs" width={820} height={452} />

Under the fur there is fat, flesh and muscle by depth, a skeleton down the spine, legs and tail, a
skull around a brain, lungs and a heart in the chest and a gut in the belly. The body is built with
gen-kit's [`RiggedVolume`](/docs/generators/api/gen-kit/gen-kit/classes/RiggedVolume/): masses
are filled per bone, parents before children so each child owns its joint, then `layerInterior`
re-roles the mass by depth and bone cores and organs are stamped afterwards.

All of it is out of sight until [`wound`](/docs/engine/api/animation/functions/wound/) carves the
model or [`sever`](/docs/engine/api/animation/functions/sever/) takes a limb off. Both work on the rest
model, so damage follows the creature through every pose baked afterwards. When a pose uncovers flesh
that was buried at rest (under a swinging haunch), the rig's `cover` table, which maps each interior
role to fur, draws it as fur; `bakePose` uses it by default. Only damage ever shows the inside.

The 19 roles are the eleven visible on a whole animal (fur, dark and light fur, belly, skin, dark
skin, claw, eye, eye shine, nose, tooth) and eight inside (fat, flesh, muscle, bone, marrow, organ,
dark organ, blood), exported as `INTERIOR`. Eyes, organs and blood carry a glossy material hint.

## Resolution rules

The proportions are chosen so the rat animates without losing the voxel look:

- nothing animated is thinner than 2 voxels, except the last third of the tail;
- at least 4 voxels between joints, so a 15 degree bend moves a limb end by a voxel;
- the detail budget goes to the head (eyes, ears, snout, incisors); the body stays simple masses;
- legs are longer than a real rat's (`legLength` 1.5): a real rat's legs hide under its belly, and a
  gait nobody can see does not read.

The default rat is about 3.5k voxels and 26 bones, and bakes a posed model in about 2 ms
(`bun run --cwd generators/creature bench` measures it).

## Sizing for a world

The presets are stylised game rats: 75 voxels nose to tail tip at `size` 1.2, which is right beside
a 10 voxels-per-metre house, but a 75 cm rat in a 1 cm world. The creature has no finer `scales`:
it is authored near that resolution already. Instead,
[`atScale(params, voxelsPerMetre)`](/docs/generators/api/gen-creature/functions/atScale/) sizes any
preset for a world with a fixed unit, keeping the presets' ratios to each other and snapping to the
0.05 grid of `shape.size` (never below 0.7):

```ts
import { atScale, generateCreature, PRESETS } from "@voxolith/gen-creature";

const rat = generateCreature(atScale(PRESETS.rat, 100), seededRandom(1)).entity;
// size 0.8: 49 voxels long, a large brown rat of about 50 cm
```

The floor of 0.7 keeps the legs two voxels thick, so below a certain scale `atScale` cannot give a
rat its real size and it comes out larger than life. For the default rat:

| voxels per metre | `shape.size` | nose to tail tip |
|---|---|---|
| 10 | 0.7 (held) | 43 voxels, about 4.3 m |
| 20 | 0.7 (held) | about 2.15 m |
| 50 | 0.7 (held) | about 0.86 m |
| 100 | 0.8 | 49 voxels, about 0.5 m: real size |

Two helpers share `atScale`'s maths, so an app can decide without hard-coding a threshold.
[`realSizeAt(params, voxelsPerMetre)`](/docs/generators/api/gen-creature/functions/realSizeAt/)
says whether `atScale` needs no clamping there, and
[`minVoxelsPerMetre(params)`](/docs/generators/api/gen-creature/functions/minVoxelsPerMetre/) is
the smallest scale where it holds: about 84.4 for the default rat, 88 for the fat rat.
`realSizeAt` is also false above about 253 voxels per metre, where the size is held at its upper
bound of 2 and the creature comes out smaller than life.

```ts
import { atScale, generateCreature, minVoxelsPerMetre, PRESETS, realSizeAt } from "@voxolith/gen-creature";

// Show rats only where they are true to size; otherwise say where they would be.
const kinds = ["rat", "grey rat", "black rat"] as const;
const rats = kinds.every((k) => realSizeAt(PRESETS[k], vpm))
  ? kinds.map((k, i) => generateCreature(atScale(PRESETS[k], vpm), seededRandom(seed + i), k).entity)
  : [];
if (!rats.length) console.log(`rats from ${Math.ceil(Math.max(...kinds.map((k) => minVoxelsPerMetre(PRESETS[k]))))} voxels per metre`);
```

The [valley example](/docs/examples/valley/) does this: its rats appear only at 100 voxels per
metre, and at 20 or 50 the HUD says "rats at 1 cm". A game that wants giant rats (the
[tutorial](/docs/tutorial/rats/)) just uses `atScale` and lets the floor win.

Small rats are where the resolution rules bite, so below size 1 the body keeps floors: lower legs and
feet stay over 2 voxels across and the first part of the tail over 1.5; there is no fat layer; and
bone and organs sit one voxel shallower, so a wound still shows them.

## Presets

| preset | differs from `rat` |
|---|---|
| `rat` | a brown rat with a pale belly |
| `grey rat` | grey coat |
| `lab rat` | white, red-eyed, barely mottled |
| `black rat` | black, slimmer, longer tail, bigger ears |
| `fat rat` | more girth, slower pace, more bounce |

## Development

```sh
bun run --cwd generators/creature verify            # binding, hidden interior, chest slice, every clip in one piece, footfalls, damage
bun run --cwd generators/creature preview species   # also: cut, clips, walk, turns; --scale 100 for the real-size rat
bun run --cwd generators/creature bench             # pose and bake cost
```

`verify` runs every clip and damage check at both the preset size and the 100 voxels-per-metre size.
